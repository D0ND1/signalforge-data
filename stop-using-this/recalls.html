Bose Recalls Lithium-Ion Battery Packs for S1 Pro and S1 Pro+ Bluetooth Speaker Systems Due to Risk of Injury from Fire and Overheating Hazards

Hazard: The recalled lithium-ion battery packs can overheat and ignite, posing a risk of injury from fire and overheating hazards.

Remedy: Consumers should remove the recalled battery packs installed in any S1 Pro and S1 Pro+ Portable Bluetooth Speaker Systems and stop using the battery packs immediately. Once the battery pack is removed, consumers may continue to use their S1 Pro and S1 Pro+ Portable Bluetooth Speakers while connected to AC power. Consumers will be asked to register and provide the affected battery pack’s serial number at www.support.bose.com/s1-battery-recall to receive a free replacement battery pack. For battery packs that are not installed in the speaker systems, consumers can simply view the serial number and provide that number to the website listed above. Consumers should dispose of the affected battery pack in accordance with local and state regulations. Note: Do not throw this recalled lithium-ion battery pack in the trash, the general recycling stream (e.g., street-level or curbside recycling bins), or used battery recycling boxes found at various retail and home improvement stores. Recalled lithium-ion batteries must be disposed of differently than other batteries because they present a greater risk of fire. Your municipal household hazardous waste (HHW) collection center may accept this recalled lithium-ion battery pack for disposal. Before taking your battery pack to a HHW collection center, contact that office ahead of time and ask whether it accepts recalled lithium-ion batteries. If it does not, contact your municipality for further guidance.

Covers: Bose S1 Pro System Battery Pack and S1 Pro+ System Battery Packs for use with Bose S1 Pro and Bose S1 Pro+ Speaker Systems.. Units: About 634,759 (In addition, about 35,400 were sold in Canada)

Got a car? Check its VIN.

A car's 17-character vehicle identification number is on the registration, the insurance card and the driver's side of the dashboard. NHTSA's free lookup lists every open recall for that exact car.

Check a VIN at NHTSA

Check if yours is affected

Vehicles

  1. Find your VIN (vehicle identification number): it is on your registration, your insurance card and the driver's side of the dashboard.
  2. Enter it on NHTSA's recall lookup. It lists every open recall for that exact vehicle.
  3. If one is open, call a dealer of that brand and follow the remedy in the notice. If the notice says park outside or do not drive, do that until it is fixed.

NHTSA recall lookup (VIN)

Products

  1. Compare the product name, model number and dates in the official notice with the label on yours.
  2. If it matches, stop using it.
  3. Follow the remedy in the notice: it names who to contact for the refund, repair or replacement.

CPSC recalls

Food and medicine

  1. Check the brand, product name, lot code and dates on the package against the notice.
  2. If it matches, don't eat or use it. The notice says whether to return it or throw it away.
  3. If you are worried about your health, contact a doctor.
